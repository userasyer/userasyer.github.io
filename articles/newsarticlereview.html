News Article Review

 While scrolling through the 'News' section on Yahoo! yesterday, an article caught my attention. An opinion post by Erna Mahyuni, the article was titled "Why are Malaysian men upset that women's coaches on public transport exist?" which greatly surprised me because I have never met a person complaining about the women's coach. In addition to that, as a male myself, I have always thought that the women's only coach is an important feature in RapidKL rail services. After reading through the article, I should say that it paints a very serious picture on how society in our lovely nation sees gender inequality.

 The article commented on the findings of a poll regarding the public's opinion on MRT Corp's women's only coach in which a staggering 50% of respondents suggested to remove the feature altogether in calls of "equality". In the point of view of Mahyuni, the response was an odd phenomenon, given that men are the reason these coaches exist, while also being the ones complaining about them. Moreover, the article critics how males who do not personally experience the distressing situation females face every day in when trying to get by using public transportations. Nevertheless, the article remains rational and manifests minimal emotional influence in explaining what women are forced to go through in public.

  In addition, the Mahyuni brought some statistics into her article which substantially solidifies her arguments regarding the rampant issues that brought to the existence of women's only coaches. Her straightforward and serious delivery communicates that the dilemma isn't just an outlier in the data, but a real dark side of our society that must be addressed Furthermore, the article continues with numerous rhetorics that question whether men realise the magnitude of this problem, or they are acting blind to downplay it.

 On the other hand, if we look at the target demographic that this article is intended for, I would say it is fitting that the article was posted on Malay Mail. Having years of recognition amongs the Malaysian public, Malay Mail is a platform that reaches the entire nation and is read by people of every age bracket. However, it is apparent that this particular article is targeted towards adults aged around 18 and above, be it male or female. The critical comments on men's ignorance of this issue is largely understood by people with sufficient maturity.

 To conclude, the opinion post was not a mere complain to talk about an insignificant nuisance when using the public transportation. It talks about a real-world issue that is still being debated by different groups of people and comes with it change in how society functions. Even if we put aside the activism or any that the person may exhibit in her other works, it is crucial to recognise the validity of her points in this article which came from her professional tone as well as willingness to find backing to her claims. Thank you for reading my review and I'll see you next time.
